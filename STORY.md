# Last Call at Tarven: Story

> A story design draft. It replaces the story parts of GAME_CONCEPT.md (sections 1, 8.2, 9 and 11) once it is approved.
> Every character is an adult (18+), and every intimate beat is still her choice. Pillar 5 of the concept stays in force.

---

## 1. Why the old story flops

| Problem | What it looks like in the game now |
|---|---|
| **The story ignores most of the game** | Corruption, lewdness, willpower, passives, brawls, after-hours duels and the brothel unlock have no meaning in the story. The endings only read gold, trust and heat. |
| **Nobody has a name** | Guests are "Farmer" and "Soldier". The Baron is a letter. Rhea, Silas, Mara, Ash and Oden only exist in the concept doc. Betraying someone with no face costs the player nothing. |
| **Last call is the same choice three times** | Every night has the same choices with the same numbers (+5 / −3 / −2, 0 / +2 / +1, +25 / −2 / −1). Selling is always the best for gold, so it isn't a real dilemma. |
| **The rumours don't connect** | Salt under the chapel, a deserter, a meeting at the mill. They are three separate threads, and what you did never comes back. |
| **Setups with no payoff** | Grandma Wren and the missing father are set up in the intro and never mentioned again. |
| **The debt is the only goal** | "Pay 150 gold in 3 nights" is a timer, not a story. She has no question to answer about herself. |

What already works and should stay: the tavern, the Baron's debt, Wren, last call, rumours overheard at tables, and the dawn report with town talk.

---

## 2. The new spine

**Grandma Wren didn't only run a tavern.** Forty years ago this building was **the Red Lantern**, the most famous pleasure house on the pass. Wren was its madam. Men told her everything in bed and over drinks, and she wrote it all down in a **red book**. Later she hung up the lantern and ran a "respectable house". She kept the book.

**The Baron doesn't want 150 gold. He wants the red book.** It proves he has been keeping the crown's toll silver from the pass for twenty years. If the crown finds out, he hangs. The debt is only a lever to get into the tavern.

**Wren didn't die in her sleep.** The Baron's tax collector, Pell, poisoned her ale while he searched the tavern for the book. He never found it, because Wren had split it up and given the pieces to the people she trusted most.

So the heroine's real question is not *"can I pay?"* It is **"whose granddaughter am I?"**:
- Wren the madam, who ruled the pass from a bed and a ledger (the **corruption** path),
- Wren the keeper, who protected the village (the **willpower and trust** path),
- or someone Wren never was.

Every system in the game now answers that question. That is how the story and the mechanics become one thing.

## 4. How each system carries the story

| System | What it means in the story |
|---|---|
| **Gold and the debt** | Act 1's clock. After Act 1 the Baron doubles it, unless you promise him the book |
| **Last call** | The main story choice of each night. Each night now has its own trade-offs, and what you choose sets a **story flag** that comes back later |
| **Rumours** | Clues to the book and to the cast. Hearing them needs you near the right table, so story and service pull on each other |
| **Trust / Heat** | Whether the village stands with you, and how close the Baron is to kicking in the door |
| **Respect** | Whether the regulars confide in you. At low respect they still talk in front of you, as if you were furniture (as planned in 7.5) |
| **Corruption, lewdness, mind stage** | The heroine becoming Wren the madam. Each mind stage unlocks one of **Wren's letters** about her own youth, and a dawn monologue |
| **Willpower** | The heroine refusing to become her. Refusing tempted touches and holding back after hours unlock Wren's **other** letters, about the keeper she became |
| **The brothel unlock** | No longer just "corruption 100". She finds **Wren's red lantern** in the cellar and chooses whether to hang it over the door. That is the Red Lantern path |
| **Brawls** | The garrison respects a keeper who can hold her own. Brawls won raise Rhea's soldiers' loyalty for the final night |
| **After-hours duels and romance** | Lovers are one way to win a piece of the book: someone who trusts you in bed lets it slip (with the existing rule that using it betrays them) |
| **House plans** | The cellar hideout holds Ash. The upstairs rooms are where Pell is searched for, lovers meet and the Red Lantern opens |
| **Journal** | Gets a **Letters** tab for Wren's letters, and a **Book** page showing which pieces you hold |

**Each piece of the book can be won four ways**, so every play style can finish the story:

| Way | Stats it uses | Price |
|---|---|---|
| **Trust** (protect them, keep their secrets) | Trust, Kindness, Protected | Heat goes up |
| **Desire** (become their lover) | Affection, Lewdness, Corruption | Respect, and a betrayal if you use what they said in bed |
| **Coin** (buy it) | Gold, Greed | Gold you needed for the debt |
| **Fists** (win their faction's respect in a brawl) | Brawl wins, Body | Injuries, the room's mood |

## 5. Act 1: The Inheritance (nights 1–3, the current build)

Act 1 keeps the three built nights. Speakers get names, each last call gets its own trade-offs, and the act ends on a twist instead of a plain ending.

### Intro (tightened)

Keep the intro, but cut the first two lines about the parents to one line. Then add the hook:
- On the bar: the Baron's letter, as now.
- Behind the bar: one shelf with a clean square in the dust, where a book used to stand. *"Grandma kept something here. Somebody's taken it. Or been looking for it."*

### Night 1: The salt

- **Who's in:** Mara Holt and a neighbour (the farmer table), Silas Crowe (the rumour guest), a soldier.
- **Rumour:** Silas tells Mara the salt is safe *"under Oden's floor"*.
- **Last call:** **Pell**, the tax collector. He looks around the bar a little too long. *"Wren and I were old friends."*

| Choice | Effect | What it changes later |
|---|---|---|
| Tell the truth: the chapel floor | +5 gold, Heat −2, Trust −3 | Oden is fined and the chapel is shut. He stops coming in. Silas's prices for bottles go up |
| Lie: send him to the quarry | Heat +1, Trust +2 | Silas leaves a crate of salt: stew sells for more on night 2 |
| Sell it | +25 gold, Trust −2 | Only three people knew. On night 2 Silas works out who talked and starts a **brawl** |
| (didn't hear) Say nothing / make something up | as now | |

### Night 2: The deserter

- **Who's in:** Ash and Silas at one table, two of Rhea's soldiers at another (the "Baron's short of silver" gossip stays: it is the first clue).
- **Rumour:** Ash whispers he deserted and crosses the border at dawn.
- **Last call:** a bounty hunter, **Garrick Thorne**.

| Choice | Effect | What it changes later |
|---|---|---|
| Tell the truth: the border, at dawn | +10 gold (the bounty), Heat −2, Trust −2 | Ash is caught. On night 3 Rhea mentions he'll hang. The father thread is lost |
| Lie: send him down the valley | Heat +1, Trust +1 | Ash gets away. He may return in Act 2 |
| **New: hide him in the cellar** (only if you protected someone on night 1, or Kindness ≥ 2) | Heat +2, Trust +2 | Ash stays in the cellar. At dawn he tells you he knew a man in the east with your face |
| Sell it | +25 gold, Trust −2 | Ash is caught |

### Night 3: The meeting

- **Who's in:** Mara with an old farmer and Silas (if he's still friendly), and Rhea's soldiers ("half the garrison wouldn't lift a sword" stays).
- **Rumour:** Mara is gathering the village at the mill the night after the debt falls due.
- **Last call:** **Captain Rhea Vance** herself, not a nameless captain. She asks, but she half hopes you won't answer.

| Choice | Effect | What it changes later |
|---|---|---|
| Tell the truth: the old mill | Heat −3, Trust −3 | Mara is arrested. **The Village Rises ending is closed for good** |
| Lie: the north road ruins | Heat +2, Trust +2 | The garrison marches into the snow |
| **New: tell Rhea where the Baron's silver goes** (only if you heard the soldiers' gossip on night 2) | Heat +1 | Rhea doesn't march. She leaves shaken. Rhea's route and the garrison's doubts begin |
| Sell it | +25 gold, Trust −2 | Mara is arrested |

### Act 1 finale: Pell at dawn

Whatever happened with the gold, Pell comes in at dawn and says the real price:

> *"Keep your hundred and fifty. The Baron wants the book your grandmother kept. Bring it to him and the debt is forgotten. Hide it, and the debt doubles."*

Then she finds **Wren's first letter**, hidden in the hollow leg of the bar stool Wren always sat on:

> *"If Pell is asking you about my book, then I'm already dead, and it wasn't my heart. Don't drink anything he pours."*

**In the current 3-night build:** Arrested stays a real game over. The other three endings play, then this scene plays as a "To be continued" teaser.

## 6. Wren's letters

Letters are the story's reward for playing the systems. Each one unlocks at a milestone and appears at dawn, found somewhere in the tavern. There are two sets, and **neither is the wrong one**.

**The madam's letters** (corruption and lewdness):

| Unlocks at | Wren writes about |
|---|---|
| First touch she lets happen | The first man who put his hand on her hip at this bar. She slapped him. *"The second time, I didn't."* |
| Mind stage 1 (warming) | How the room changes when they know you might say yes |
| First after-hours duel | What men say afterwards, and why she started writing it down |
| Mind stage 2 (eager) | The Red Lantern's best years, and what it cost her with the chapel |
| Mind stage 3 (filthy) | Where the red lantern is hidden in the cellar. *"Hang it, and they'll know what this house is again."* (This unlocks the brothel choice) |

**The keeper's letters** (willpower, trust and respect):

| Unlocks at | Wren writes about |
|---|---|
| First tempted touch she refuses | *"Wanting it and saying no anyway is the strongest thing you'll ever do behind this bar."* |
| A brawl won | The night she threw the Baron's father out into the snow |
| Trust reaches half | Why she took the lantern down |
| Protecting someone at last call three times | The names she never wrote in the book |
| Willpower kept above half for 5 nights in a row | Your grandfather, and why she chose him |

**Story letters** (fixed beats): Act 1 finale (Pell), the Act 2 midpoint (proof of the poison), and the Act 3 letter about Father.

## 7. Act 2: The Red Book (nights 4–9)

- The debt is 300, or "forgotten" if she promises Pell the book. Pell visits every few nights and his patience shrinks.
- **Upstairs rooms** open in House Plans.
- Each night carries a rumour about one piece of the book. Getting each piece is a short route with that character, won through one of the four ways in section 4.
- **The Red Lantern choice** is offered once the madam's last letter is found.
- **Midpoint (night 6–7):** Oden (or Silas, if Oden was betrayed) tells her what he saw the night Wren died. She now knows Pell killed her. New last-call choice that night: **pour Pell a drink of your own**. Choosing it removes Pell for good, and the Baron comes in person sooner (Heat jumps).
- **Story flags from Act 1 come back:** a shut chapel means Oden's piece must be bought from the Baron's men; a caught Ash means Father's pages are lost unless Rhea's route reaches him in the keep's cells.

## 8. Act 3: Last Call (nights 10–14)

- Ash (or a letter from the east) brings word of Father and the last pages.
- **The final night is a normal service night with Baron Hesk at the back table.** Every guest in the room is someone your choices sent there: Rhea's soldiers, Silas's crew, Mara's farmers. At last call the Baron asks for the book, and the ending depends on what you hold and who is standing in your tavern.

## 9. Endings

| Ending | Needs | Epilogue |
|---|---|---|
| **The Village Rises** | Mara free, Trust high, the book given to the village | The crown's inspector hangs the Baron. The tavern becomes the village's meeting hall |
| **The Red Lantern** | The lantern hung, Corruption high, the book kept | The pass's most notorious house, and the Baron can't touch it. Leverage, never payment: she doesn't sleep with the Baron for it |
| **Wren's Heir** | The book kept, Informed high, Trust middling | She becomes the spymaster of the pass, exactly like her grandmother |
| **Bought and Sold** | The book given to the Baron | Debt forgiven, tavern kept. Everyone she protected pays for it |
| **Paid in Full** | Gold ≥ debt, no book | The tavern is hers, and the Baron is still watching |
| **Lovers' endings** (one per route) | That lover's route finished | Their faction stands with her at the climax |
| **Arrested** | Heat maxed | Irons |
| **Foreclosed** | Gold short, no book | She walks down the mountain |
| **Lost to the Night** | Willpower spent most nights, mind stage 3, Respect and Gold very low, no lantern hung | A cautionary epilogue: the room owns her name, not her |
