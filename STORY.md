# Last Call at Tarven: Story

> The story design document. It replaces the story parts of GAME_CONCEPT.md (sections 1, 8.2, 9 and 11) once it is approved.
> **Draft 2.26, 20 Nov 2026.** See the changelog at the bottom. **New in 2.26:** the five houses are a ladder. Each is one step further along in the trade, and each beaten boss warns her what the next house has that she doesn't, which is what the win unlocks (section 7, "The ladder"). **New in 2.25:** each beaten boss shows her how the feature they unlocked works, in a short hands-on guide (section 7, "The boss shows her how"). **New in 2.24:** rivals are bosses. Each one's climb has 3 to 5 **stages**: at each fan mark the boss comes in for a comic scene and sends a mini-boss to brawl her that night, and the last stage is the boss's own fight. Beating a boss is what **unlocks the next feature** (staff, the feed, the garrison, the rooms and the guild, the full house). The acts still run on fans as before. The fan targets are about 5× longer (section 7).

**House rules stay in force.** Tarven is an adult game, and it's written **raw and hard**, as explicit as Karryn's Prison: cocks, cunts, cum and all of it, said plainly, by her, the guests and the room. Every character is an adult (18+), and the game says so. Consent is the player's switch (GAME_PATH.MD, the consent rule, which is the source of truth): with **Consent on** (the default) every touch asks first, Slap and Pull away always work, being too drunk blocks every yes, and a lost brawl only offers, with Stop always there. With **Consent off** it plays like Karryn's Prison: in a brawl their hands land and they tear her clothes off, at drink 8 the table can happen whether she's ready or not, a lost brawl just happens with no Stop, and easing the ache at night can draw someone in. Whatever the switch, **nothing ever happens to her while she's passed out**, and nobody roaring drunk takes her.

> **The main goal is popularity.** She starts with one cold tavern and a debt, and aims to make it **the most popular house on the pass**. She beats the rival taverns, buys them up, and decides whether to work with the bosses she beat, the Baron included. The red book is the story underneath. It explains why the Baron wants her tavern, and it is the leverage the top house gets to use.

> **Fans drive the story, nights don't.** Every story beat, from the first rumour to the final Last Call, fires on her **fan count**. Nights keep passing and bring small things (section 7, "What nights do"), but no night count ever forces a beat or ends the run. A player who wants the ending can push for fans and reach it fast. A player who wants to explore can stay, try the other choices, and replay to see what else happens.

> **Current scope: Chapter 1, Tarven.** Build and balance the village first: five houses to beat, Act 1 along the way, and the chapter ends when she is first in Tarven (section 7, "Tarven first"). The pass, Acts 2 and 3 and the Lantern come later.

## 2. The origin

> **World note (2026-11-17):** the setting is now medieval with modern things (`WORLD.md`). People have phones, so the story keeps paper on purpose: every message goes through the Baron's relay stone, Pell reads them, and anyone with a secret writes it down instead. Wren's letters, the ledger and the red book stay paper. Where the origin says Father's "letters stopped", read "messages, then letters": out east there's no relay.

> **Comedy pass (2026-11-17, the user's call):** Tarven is an adult **comedy** (`WORLD.md` §7d), and the story isn't very dark any more. What changed:
> - **Nobody murdered Wren.** She died in her sleep, as the intro says: old, happy, and smiling. Pell only snuck in the night after to search the house for the book, in his nightshirt, and Oden saw him.
> - **Pell is a petty, cowardly clerk**, not a killer. He forged the debt to get the book, and he's terrified of Old Hollis, the dwarves' accountant Hero.
> - **Baron Hesk is greedy and vain.** The stolen toll silver paid for a giant golden statue of himself, hidden in the keep. It's the thing he'd most hate the Crown to see.
> - **Father isn't in a cell.** He went east with the last pages, got lost, joined an orc clan of the Khanate of Grask, married into it, and is too embarrassed to write home.
> - **The war in the east stays far away**, in the background. Nobody dies on screen. The Hero of the East is the one real danger.
> - **The Crown comes in person:** King Edmund and Queen Seraphine join the auditor's tour in disguise (`WORLD.md` §8). Pleasing them is a new way to win the Act 3 plot.
> - **Dirty choices are funny now, not cruel:** laxatives in a rival's ale, not poison; getting Pell drunk enough to confess on Zane's feed, not killing him.

## 3. The spine

**On the surface (the goal the player chases):** climb from a cold, indebted tavern to the most popular house on the pass, then own the pass's houses. Section 7 covers how.

**Underneath (why it matters):**

- **The Baron doesn't want 150 gold. He wants the red book.** It proves twenty years of stolen toll silver, and the crown's auditor is coming this winter. The debt is only a lever to get into the tavern.
- **The debt is forged.** Pell wrote the note after Wren died. Later, her letters show the handwriting doesn't match, which becomes a clue.
- **Pell was in the house the night Wren died.** He didn't hurt her (she died in her sleep, smiling); he came to search for the book, and Oden saw him climbing out of the window in his nightshirt.
- **Wren split the book instead of sending it to the crown,** because half the village is in it too. Exposing the Baron would ruin them as well.

So the heroine's real question is not *"can I pay?"* It is **"whose granddaughter am I?"**:
- Wren the madam, who ruled the pass from a bed and a ledger,
- Wren the keeper, who protected the village,
- or someone Wren never was.

> **Why "top tavern" matters to the plot.** The crown's auditor arrives in Act 3 and lodges at **the best house on the pass**. Whoever hosts him decides what he hears. Being the top tavern is how the book reaches the crown, so the race for fans and the main plot become one goal.

## 4b. Her arc: sheltered to known

The systems already track how far she's come: the six desire meters, Lewdness, the records, the passives, the touch counts by guest kind and the Consent switch. The writing doesn't use them enough yet. This section turns those numbers into a slow personal arc in the style of Karryn's Prison. It starts with a sheltered woman who has never been kissed and ends with the keeper the whole pass talks about. **It adds no new core mechanic.** Every stage is read from data the game already saves.

### The four stages, and Legend past them

The stage is a **writing layer** on top of the mind stage (`lewd.ts`). It reads **Lewdness only**, so section 15 fix 1 must land first. Corruption doesn't change her stage. It changes the stage's *tone* (see "Corruption colours the stage" below).

| Stage | Gate (reads existing data) | Slut Level | Mind stage | Typical firsts by now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sheltered** | Lewdness 0–19 | 0–1 | 0 | Hands, face, hair, shoulder. No kiss, nothing on her chest, nothing below the waist |
| **Curious** | Lewdness 20–53 | 2–5 | 1 (warming) | Her first kiss, first chest, first rear, first lap or thigh. The first invitation can come (Lewdness 25) |
| **Willing** | Lewdness 54–106, **or** 3 nights shared upstairs (*The Back Stairs*) | 5–10 | 2 (eager) | Upstairs, After Hours, a peek or a proper look, clothes lost |

| **Legend** (past Known, lasts, can be come back from) | Known **and** Lewdness 160+ (`LEGEND_AT`), **then** 3 wild nights in a row (6+ touches let a night, `LEGEND_WILD`, `LEGEND_RISE`). ### Legend: the comic stage past Known (decided 18 Nov 2026)

The open question in CORRUPTION_TROPES.md ("a mind-break top stage") is answered **yes, played for laughs**. Past Known she can become **Legend**: cheerfully shameless, the taproom chanting her name, a song with a filthy second verse, a drum. It's the mind-break trope (with the chant as the brainwashing) on the Aftermath beat, and it's comedy, not dark: nobody is hurt, she isn't lost, she's just forgotten what blushing was for.

- **Reaching it (late).** She must be Known, with Lewdness 160+ (`LEGEND_AT`, well past Known's 107), and then have **3 wild nights in a row** (`LEGEND_RISE`): a wild night is one where she let 6 or more touches (`LEGEND_WILD`). Any other night starts the count again. The count, and whether she's a Legend, is saved (`story.legend`, `LegendState` in `lewd.ts`); older saves load as never having been one.
- **Coming back.** At Legend, **3 quiet nights in a row** (`LEGEND_FALL`) bring her back to Known. A quiet night is one where she kept her willpower above half all night (the same test as Wren's keeper letter) and let at most 1 touch (`LEGEND_QUIET_LETS`). A day at the chapel (any chapel action: Oden, a candle for Wren, confession) counts one more on top of that night. A wild night starts the count again. If she stops being Known (or under 160), she's back at once. She can rise again later, and each rise and each return gets its card. This uses the doc's "way back": conditioning is broken by nights off, brainwashing by the chapel.
- **Cards** (dawn report, `legendCardLines`): the first rise is *The chant* (her dawn line "(They chanted my name till the rafters shook. I took a bow. I wasn't wearing anything to bow in.)", then the beat, then her Consent-on or Consent-off line); every later rise is *By popular demand*; coming back is *Even legends have nights off*.
- **What changes.** Words only: her touch asides (`flavorLine`: half the time her cheek and the room's chant, else the body's words with a grin), town talk (her own passing lines, replies and chats; everything written for Known still applies), a few town places (Oden, the bakehouse, the yard, the art class), the stage name by the Force bar and in the journal's Private page (with how close she is to rising or coming back), and three passives: *Tarven's Legend* (first rise), *By Popular Demand* (second rise) and *Even Legends Rest* (coming back, Composure). At the bar she talks in her Known voice.
- **Consent, unchanged.** Legend changes no check. Consent on still asks every time, Stop always works, too drunk still blocks every yes, and nothing happens to her while she's passed out. Her Consent-on line says it: "Stop still works. I just haven't needed it since Tuesday."
- **Staff and the Lantern's girls** have their own Legend past their Known: Lewdness 140+ (`STAFF_LEGEND_AT`) and 4 nights worked in a row (a girl: 2+ bookings on a lit night), back to Known after 2 nights off in a row. Each has a Legend line (`STAFF_STAGE_LINES`, `JOB_STAGE_LINES`), a look key (`house_legend`), a card when they rise and one when they come back. They always chose it and can always say no.

#### The same touch at each stage, Consent on and off

### Writing rules for this arc

- **Raw and explicit, by her stage.** Every line can be as hard as Karryn's Prison's. What changes with her stage is *her* voice, not how explicit the game is: Sheltered she's shocked and swears she won't, Curious she's flustered and turned on, Willing she asks for it by name, Known she begs for it and loves it (KP's experience levels). Guests are crude from the start.
- **Consent on: never write a touch after a no.** Consent off (KP): it can be her being overpowered, and she fights it in her own voice; written as Karryn's Prison writes it.
- **Too drunk is always a no with Consent on,** at every stage, in every voice (`TOO_DRUNK_TO_ALLOW`). Passed out is a no whatever the switch.
- **A refusal is never "backsliding".** At Willing and Known, saying no is written as confidence, not innocence returning.
- **Stages are earned by the player's yeses.** Nothing in the story pushes her stage up. The story only reacts to where she is.

## 7. Popularity: the main goal

**Fame** (already built) is how far her name travels, and it brings new faces. **Fans** are the guests who keep coming back to her tavern. **Popularity** is her place on the Pass Ledger, ranked by fans. She competes with the rivals for fans, not fame.

### The story moves by fans, not by nights

The whole story, **Act 1 included**, is **measured in fans**. A beat fires at the start of the first night after she reaches its fan count. Beats fire in order, at most one a night, and each fires only once. So the fastest a player can go is one beat a night, and there is no slowest.

| Stage | Fans | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| **Chapter 1: Tarven** (Act 1 inside it) | 0 → 2,500 | **The current scope.** Beat the five houses in Tarven, each a boss with its stages, and become the first house in the village. Act 1 (the salt, the deserter, the meeting, Pell's price) plays along the way. See "Tarven first" below |
| *Later:* **Climb the pass** (Act 2) | 2,500 → 7,500 | Win a name on the pass, collect the book, face the pass rivals |
| **Top of the pass** (Act 3) | 7,500 → about 10,000 | Beat the rivals on the Ledger. The Baron and the auditor come to the top house |

**Two tracks, kept apart (2.24):**
- **The story track (the acts):** every act beat fires on fans alone, as before. A boss stage she hasn't won never holds back a story beat, and a story beat never holds back a boss.
- **The rival track (the features):** each rival is a boss with stages along the climb to its fans (a scene and a mini-boss brawl at each mark, the boss's own fight last), and **each boss beaten unlocks the next feature** (the table in "Tarven first"). This is how the player knows what to do next: the HUD and the journal always show the next stage, the fans it needs, and what beating the boss unlocks.

**How long it takes (2.24):** the old targets (100 to 500) were done in 8 to 12 good nights, which is too short for a game. The targets are now about 5× longer, with the gaps growing, because each feature she unlocks earns her more fans a night (staff serve faster, the rooms and the guild bring more guests). The aim is **8 to 10 nights per boss, more with lost stages, and 40 to 50 nights for Chapter 1**. Needs playtesting (open question 1c).

### Replay

The story is short when pushed and wide when explored, so a second run should feel different:

- **The journal remembers across runs:** endings found, letters read and Ledger victims are kept in a **"What happened"** page, with blanks for the ones not seen yet. This tells the player there's more to find.
- **Four kinds of heroine** (section 4) and three crowns (below) give each run a different shape.

### The ladder: each house is one step up the trade (2.26)

The five houses aren't just bigger and bigger numbers. **Each one is a step further along in the business than the last**, and that step is its **edge**. The first is a tavern just like hers. Each one after has one more thing she hasn't got yet. When she beats a house, its boss, humbled, tells her straight what the next house has that she doesn't (*"If you want to last in this trade, you'll need…"*). That is exactly what the win unlocks, and the beaten boss's guide teaches it ("The boss shows her how", below). So she always walks into the next war with the same tools as the house she's fighting, and she always knows why she's fighting it.

**The comedy tropes:**
- **The rival who becomes the reluctant teacher:** every boss she beats ends up teaching her, sulking or proud.

### A rival war: how a boss is beaten (2.24)

Every house is beaten the same way, so the player learns it once on Hollin and knows what to do for every boss after. **The boss lasts the whole climb to its fans, not a fight at the end.** Its stages sit at fan marks along the way: at each one the boss walks in for a comic scene, then sends a **mini-boss** to brawl her that night. The last stage, at the house's own fan count, is the boss's own fight. A boss takes about 8 to 10 nights of play, more with lost stages.

**1. A stage mark (her fans reach it).** For Hollin: 100, 200 and 300 fans. The next morning, the boss comes to her door, or to the bar as the doors open.

**2. The boss's scene (comedy).** A short dialogue card in the house voice, Tarven style (a medieval adult comedy with phones, WORLD.md): the boss brags, sulks, threatens, makes a bet or tries to bribe her, and she can answer back. Her answers are flavour with a small effect (a little fame or respect, or the mini-boss starting angrier), never a story flag. The scene names tonight's mini-boss and what's riding on it. Each boss gets one scene per stage, and each scene is funnier and more desperate than the last.

**3. The mini-boss brawl (that night).** The mini-boss walks in with the guests during service; a toast card names it (*"Hollin's war, stage 1 of 3: Wat and Will, the cider twins"*), the room clears a space, and it's a brawl with every brawl rule (and the consent rule, GAME_PATH.MD). She still serves and earns fans on the night.
- **Mini-bosses are named and have a gimmick:** a foe or a pair with a prefix (`foeprefix.ts`), a few more hit points than a guest, one signature move or line, and a brag before and a sulk after. They get stronger stage by stage.
- **Losing a stage** loses the night, not the war: the stage stays where it was, and the mini-boss comes back the next night to try again (*"Back for seconds, innkeeper?"*). She takes **Bruised** as for any lost brawl, the house poaches 2 walk-ins, and she loses 10 fans (they saw her go down). Won stages never reset, and nothing ends the run.
- **She can ask for a night off.** By day, from the Ledger: *"Not tonight."* The mini-boss waits a night, and the house sends its talk and poachers instead (the built rival moves).
- **Stages go in order.** The next stage needs both its fan mark and the last stage won. Her fans keep counting past a stage she hasn't won; the next mark's scene simply waits until it is.
- **Between stages** the house fights back on some nights, as built: poaching walk-ins, talk in the square, a hired troublemaker. The boss's lines turn up in the gossip.
- **Dirty work by day** (the "Beat it dirty" column) takes **one mini-boss off** the war, at the usual price: corruption, heat and a Ledger entry with the boss as the victim. Once a war, never the boss's own fight.
- **The room takes sides.** Her fans of the house's tag cheer, and once she has a doorman (after Hollin, STAFF.md) he takes one foe off her in a stage with more than one. *(Later: fans who join the brawl on her side.)*

**4. The boss's own fight (the last stage, at the house's fans).** The longest scene, then the boss in person, or the boss and their champion. Win it, and the next morning the boss comes in beaten, with the **Partner or Ruin** card as before, gives up their clue, and the **unlock** card pops (`unlocktoast.ts`): what's new, and where to find it. Then **the boss shows her how it works**, step by step, in their own voice (below, "The boss shows her how").

#### The boss's scenes: Hollin, as the example

The house voice, played for laughs. Every boss has the same shape: the first scene is a warning, the middle ones get more desperate, and the last is the boss putting on their best (worst) show.

**Stage 1 (100 fans): "A friendly word."**
> Hollin stands in the doorway, turning his hat in his hands. "Morning, Miss. Lovely place. Lovely stew, I hear. *Everyone* hears. My barn's quiet as a church on market day."
> "Is that a threat, Hollin?"
> "A threat? Me?" He looks wounded. "I'm a seed lender. I don't threaten. I *remind*." He nods at the window, where two enormous lads are waving. "That's Wat and Will. They've come for a drink tonight. Several drinks. On your tab, I expect."

*Her answers:* **"They'll pay like everyone else."** (respect +1) / **"Tell them the first round's free."** (fame +1; the twins start merrier, and a little weaker) / **"Send them. I've washed bigger pots than them."** (the twins start angry, and the room's watching: fame +2 if she wins).

**Stage 2 (200 fans): "Terms."**
> Hollin's back, with a ledger. "I've done sums, Miss. If my farmers drink at yours, they can't pay me. If they can't pay me, I can't pay the Baron. And if I can't pay the Baron..." He draws a finger across his throat. "So really, by drinking your ale, they're killing me. You're a murderer, is what I'm saying."
> "That's not how sums work."
> "It's how *my* sums work." He sniffs. "Mother Gorse is coming tonight. She's seventy. If you hit her, the whole valley will know. If you don't..." He shrugs. "She's got a very hard stick."

**Stage 3 (300 fans): "The old way."**
> Hollin rolls up his sleeves, then thinks better of it and rolls them down again. "Right. Tonight we settle it the old way. The Cider Barn against your... your *establishment*." Behind him, Big Bram ducks under the door frame. And keeps ducking. "Bram'll do the settling. I'll do the supervising."
> "And if I win?"
> "Then you can have my regulars. And my barn cat, who's never liked me anyway."

**When he's beaten:** the Partner or Ruin card as built, opening with his built line (*"Half my regulars drink at yours now. So. What's it to be between us?"*), then the ladder's warning (2.26): *"You beat me because there's one of me and I'm fifty-five. Tansy's got lads. You want to stand in this trade, you need hands."* Then the unlock: *"Take Bram on, if he'll have you. He eats like three men, but he works like four."* **New: Staff.** Then Hollin walks her to the notice board and shows her how hiring works (his guide, below).

The other four bosses keep the same shape. Tansy gossips and brings pies with a side of guilt, Pike sends formal written challenges with the spelling wrong, Agnes reads out every rule she's broken (with dates), and Jory is charming, then furious, then rings his bell at her.

### The boss shows her how (2.25)

A new feature never just appears with a toast. **The boss who opened it teaches her to use it.** The morning after the boss's own fight, right after the Partner or Ruin card and the unlock toast, the beaten boss says *"Right. I'll show you how it's done, since it's yours now"* and walks her through it. It's a short hands-on guide, not a wall of text.

**How a guide plays:**
- **A few steps, 3 to 5,** each one thing to do. The boss says a line (their portrait on the talk card, the dialogue box as for any talk). An **arrow** points at what to use: the target arrow over a spot in the world (the notice board, the garrison gate, the stairs), or a **glow** on the menu row or button to press. The step waits until she actually does it, then the boss reacts and gives the next one.
- **Learning by doing:** every step is the real feature, not a mock-up. Whatever she does in the guide counts: a hire is a real hire, a post is a real post.
- **Short:** a guide fits in one day, a few minutes of play. A step that only works at night (the doorman at work, a room let at last call) waits for that night, and the boss turns up for it (*"Told you I'd be back. Watch him work."*).
- **Her own gates still apply.** A guide shows the way to her own choices and never makes them for her. Tansy only mentions the Lantern's road. Jory shows her the bar stage, but dancing is hers to say yes to (the consent rule, GAME_PATH.MD). Nothing lewd happens in a guide unless she starts it.
- **Skip or replay:** an *"I've got it"* button at every step ends the guide, and the feature is still unlocked. The journal's Ledger page has **"Ask (boss) to show you again"** for every house she has beaten.
- **Saved mid-way:** a guide she's partway through is saved with the day, and picks up at the same step after a load.
- **Old saves:** a house already fallen isn't guided at once. Its "Ask (boss) to show you" waits in the journal.

**Partner or Ruin changes the teacher, never the lesson.** Every step is taught either way, so a player who ruins a house still learns the feature.
- **Partner:** the boss is cheerful, a bit proud, and over-explains. At the end they add a small **partner's gift** (below).
- **Ruin:** the boss teaches it anyway, sulking. They need the work, or it's the price of their grudge. The lines are sarcastic and there is no gift. Pike sends Corporal Wace instead, who reads the regulations aloud the whole way through.

### Working with the Baron

Once she is the top house and holds enough of the book, she has a choice the old story never gave: **beat the Baron, or work with him.**

- **The price:** someone has to answer for Wren. Pell is handed over, or the partnership is built on her grandmother's murder (a Ledger entry). The village and Rhea see it as a betrayal unless she uses the partnership to protect them.
- **The leverage stays hers.** She never gives up the book, which is the difference from Bought and Sold. And the house rule still holds: he is a partner, never a client.

#### The girls

The girls are adult workers who chose the work (section 12, L3). More ask to join as the Lantern's fame grows, or when a rival house falls. Each girl has:

| Stat | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Appeal** | How many guests ask for her |
| **Charm** | How happy guests come down. Satisfaction, tips, Girls fans |
| **Stamina** | How many bookings she can take before she needs a night off |
| **Loyalty** | Whether she stays, defends the house, or walks to the Velvet House |
| **Knack** | The guest faction she's best with (soldiers, nobles, adventurers and so on) |
| **Corruption** | How crooked she has become. It drifts toward the madam's (see "Corruption spreads" below) |
| **Resolve** | How slowly her corruption drifts. Stubborn girls hold out longer |
| **Lewdness** | Her own slut level, worked out from her own nights the way the heroine's is (see "Her lewdness" below) |

**Fixed and not negotiable:** any girl can refuse any guest, and can quit. The madam's choices are about **money and protection**, never consent:

| House rule she sets | Fair choice | Greedy choice |
|---|---|---|
| **The cut** | Half to the girl | Most to the house. More gold, loyalty down, corruption up |
| **Rough guests** | Thrown out (needs a doorman or a brawl) | Kept for the coin. Loyalty down, corruption up |
| **Rest** | A night off when stamina runs out | Worked tired. Charm down, a girl may fall ill |
| **Drunk guests** | Turned away from the stairs | Let up. More bookings, more trouble |

A fair house earns less early on but keeps its girls, and loyal girls stand with her in the Velvet war and on the final night. A greedy house earns more and loses them, which leads toward The Lantern Burns.

#### Corruption spreads

The madam sets the tone of the house. **As her corruption rises, her girls' corruption rises with it.**

- **The drift:** every lit night, each girl's corruption moves toward the madam's. It closes about a tenth of the gap, slowed by her Resolve. Rising is easy and falling is slow: when the madam is cleaner than a girl, the girl's corruption falls at half speed.
- **Greedy house rules push harder:** each lit night with a greedy rule (a big cut, rough guests kept, tired girls worked) adds corruption to every girl directly, on top of the drift.
- **Starting points:** Tamsin starts shady (she learned at the Velvet House), Odile honest, and Bryn honest but with low Resolve.

A girl uses the same four tiers as the madam (section 8), and each tier changes what she does:

| Her tier | What she does | Good for the madam | Bad for the madam |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Honest** | Works fair, refuses dirty jobs | Loyal, and guests trust her | If the madam is crooked or worse, she may quit |
| **Shady** | Upsells drinks, hears more than she says | Tips up, a few Secrets fans | Little yet |
| **Crooked** | Lifts purses from drunk guests and sells clients' secrets to the madam. Takes dirty jobs against rivals | Gold, information (Informed), a ready hand for sabotage | Heat up, robbed guests leave as fans of a rival, and each job goes in the Ledger with her name beside the madam's |
| **Rotten** | Skims from the house, and sells the madam's secrets to Pell or Sabine | Ruthless and fearless | She may walk out to start her own house and take fans with her, the way Sabine left Wren |

**It reaches the other houses too.** Managers and partner bosses in the houses she owns (section 7) drift toward her corruption the same way. A rotten owner ends up running a rotten chain.

**Turning a house around** is slow. She has to bring her own corruption down (section 8) and switch to fair house rules, and then the girls follow at half speed. She can also let a rotten girl go, which costs her fans and risks a grudge.

**Endings:** a rotten house that turns on its madam is **The Lantern Burns**. A rotten house that stays loyal out of fear is part of **The New Baron**.

#### Her lewdness (each girl's slut level)

Each girl has her own **Lewdness**, built like the heroine's (PASSIVES_DESIGN.md, "Lewdness"). It isn't raised directly. It's **worked out from her own records**:
- 1 for every 3 bookings she has taken,
- 2 for every booking she enjoyed (a booking with high satisfaction and a guest of her knack),
- the lewd points of her own passives (a small set for the girls, earned from her own records, such as *Garrison Favourite* or *Red Room Regular*).

Her mind stage uses the same thresholds as the heroine's (warming, eager, filthy), and it changes how she works:

| Her stage | What changes |
|---|---|
| **0 (new)** | Takes plain bookings only. Shy with rough or drunk guests |
| **1 (warming)** | Appeal up. Flirts at the tables and brings in bookings herself |
| **2 (eager)** | Takes bolder bookings at higher prices. Asks to work dark nights too |
| **3 (filthy)** | The house's star, and the most-asked-for girl. Stamina drains faster, and Sabine will pay a lot to poach her |

**How the madam's house shapes it, without forcing it:**
- **The house's tone:** the madam's own mind stage sets how bold the Lantern is. A bolder house draws bolder guests and pays more, so girls gain experience faster, if they choose to take the bookings.
- **Who applies:** a famous Lantern attracts girls who start with higher lewdness. A quiet one attracts beginners.
- **Never forced:** at any lewdness, a girl can still refuse any guest and can quit. The madam can't raise a girl's lewdness with an order, only offer her the work.

**Lewdness and corruption together** give four kinds of girl, the same way they do for the heroine:

| | Low corruption | High corruption |
|---|---|---|
| **Low lewdness** | **The quiet one.** Honest, shy, loyal. Steady but small takings | **The spy.** Takes few bookings, hears everything, sells it |
| **High lewdness** | **The house favourite.** Honest and bold. The best fans come for her | **The next Sabine.** Bold, rotten, ambitious. The biggest earner, and the most likely to leave and start her own house |

#### A Lantern night

1. Service runs as normal. She still pours, cooks and cleans.
2. A lustful guest asks for company. A **booking card** opens: which girl (her knack and stamina), which room, and the price.
3. The room's door lamp lights while it's busy. Bookings happen off screen, with a timer and no scenes.
4. The guest comes down and pays. Satisfaction depends on the girl's charm and the room. A happy guest may become a **Girls** fan.
5. **Trouble** can break out: a rough guest (doorman or brawl), a jealous fan, Oden at the door, Sabine's runner poaching, or an unlicensed raid when heat is high.
6. **Her own invitations** work as they do now (the upstairs invitations in PASSIVES_DESIGN.md). Being the madam doesn't oblige her to anything. *Mistress of the Upstairs Room* and *The Back Stairs* still count only her own nights.

At dawn, the report adds a Lantern line: bookings, room takings, the girls' cut, each girl's mood, and any trouble.

#### What it does to her stats
