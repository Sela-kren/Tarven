## 1. The one-line world

**An adult comedy** (section 7d) **in a medieval fantasy world of many kingdoms and peoples (humans, elves, dwarves, orcs, beastfolk, goblins) where everyday life is modern.** Castles, barons, knights in armour, adventurers' guilds, gods and monsters, the way an isekai world works. But people carry **phones**, wear **modern clothes**, take selfies, leave reviews, order takeaway and swipe on dating apps. A knight in plate checks his phone at the bar, next to an orc mercenary and a goblin selling phone charms. A farmer's daughter in a crop top and jeans sells turnips under the keep's walls, and posts the price on the feed.

**The short version:** *life is modern, power is medieval.* What people wear, buy, eat, say and do for fun is modern. Who rules, who fights, who you pray to and what lives in the ruins is medieval.

The game never explains it as strange. To the people of Tarven it's just how the world is.

## 2b. Modern daily life (the modern lean, 2026-11-18, draft)

**People in Tarven live like people in a small modern town.** They just live under a baron, next to a keep and a mountain full of monsters.

**In town:**
- **Shops** sit in the old timber and stone buildings, with glass shopfronts and glowing mana signs set into medieval walls. Goblin's Phone & Repair, a café that does iced coffee, a boutique, a tattoo and piercing parlour, a gym where the garrison lifts, a laundromat.
- **Takeaway:** goblin delivery runners carry hot food up the pass road on foot, phone in one hand.

- **Reviews:** every shop, tavern and blacksmith has a star rating on the feed. One bad review from Zane (section 7c) and the whole town knows.

- **Fitness and looks:** gym selfies, skincare, hair dye, piercings, tattoos, nails. Adventurers post "monster hunt day" workout videos.

**In her tavern:**
- Still timber, hearth, casks and stew pot (section 6), but run like a modern bar: a **drinks menu board**, **cocktails** next to the ale, **happy hour**, **karaoke night**, a **DJ night** with the mana speaker, **string lights** and a **glowing sign** over the door.

Grown women with money or rank wear long, fitted one-piece dresses, like the elegant older women in manhwa: tight through the body, long to the floor, cut high up the leg, with heels. It's the fancy end of modern fashion, and a big help for the variety rule.

## 3. How the modern things work

The isekai answer: **everything modern runs on mana-stones**. Small glowing crystals power phones, lights and cold boxes. They're mined and charged in the lowlands and sold up the pass.

The mana-stones turn the modern things into story:
- **The Baron owns the relay stone on the pass.** Every phone message in Tarven goes through his keep, and Pell reads them. So people who have secrets **write them on paper**. That's why Wren kept a paper ledger and wrote letters, and why the red book is a book. "Never text what you can whisper" is a Tarven saying.
- **Mana-stones cost money.** Farmers have cheap, cracked phones. Nobles have fine ones. A dead phone is a poor man's problem.
- **The high pass has bad signal.** Up on the quarry road and past the gate, phones go quiet. Fugitives and smugglers like it there.
- **Monsters and old ruins mess with mana.** In the ruins on the north road, phones die. Adventurers still carry swords, because a sword never runs out of charge.

**Guns:** none, or rare and useless (mana-stones can't power them, and powder fails in the mountain cold). Knights and swords stay the real power.

The world has **many kingdoms and many peoples**: humans, elves, dwarves, orcs, beastfolk and goblins. **All of them are people.** They think, trade, love, lie and drink. Goblins and orcs aren't monsters to be cleared out of a cave; they're neighbours, rivals and customers. (The real monsters are in section 12.)

**Every one of them is an adult when they appear in the game (18+), and looks it.** That matters most for the short peoples. | **Goblins** | Short, wiry, green, big ears, sharp grins | Clever, quick-talking, shameless hagglers, mad for gadgets | Merchants and smugglers; always selling something; pay in gadgets |

**What each people thinks of sex** (for the writing):
- **Elves:** slow and elaborate. They court for a season, so a quick tumble in a tavern is a scandal to them (and a thrill).
- **Dwarves:** private behind closed doors, boasting in the bar afterwards.
- **Orcs:** open and direct. They ask straight out, take a no straight, and don't understand shame.
- **Beastfolk:** depends on the kin. Heat seasons are spoken of openly.
- **Goblins:** everything is a deal, including that, and they'll haggle over it.
- **Humans:** everything in between, and the Church of Veyra knows it.

**For the game:**
- **Variety rule:** the peoples are the best answer to "never one body recoloured". A party of four can be a human, an elf, an orc and a goblin.
- **Factions keep their jobs, the peoples mix:** an orc soldier in the garrison, a goblin smuggler in Silas's crew, an elf noble, a dwarf adventurer, a fox beastfolk fugitive.
- **Art, cheapest first:**
  1. Elves: ears on the existing heads.
  2. Beastfolk: ears and a tail, like the bunny set's.
  3. Orcs: skin tones, tusks, the heavy body sliders.
  4. Dwarves: the existing bodies short and broad, plus beards.
  5. Goblins: a new small adult body, the biggest job.
- **Prejudice is a story tool:** some guests sneer at orcs or goblins. How she treats them moves those peoples' fans, and Respect with the bigots.

## 7d. Tone: an adult comedy

**Last Call at Tarven is a comedy.** Crude, absurd and messed up, and sexual. The world takes ridiculous things completely seriously: a church of sex, a goblin selling phones to a knight, a kingdom's Hero living on the Crown's tab in a bar.

## 9. Titles in public (no status plates)

**There are no status plates**: nobody can see anyone's stats or skills. Her stats, skills and passives are hers alone, in her journal.

**Titles are different:** a title (`titles.ts`, Karryn's Prison style) is **what people start calling her**, so some titles spread out into the town. They travel by gossip and the town feed, the way any reputation does.

- **Private titles** only work for her, as now (the bonus and the effect in `titles.ts`).
- **Public titles** also change how the world treats her while she wears one. Guests use the title to her face, strangers in the street know it, and some places open or close.

| Kind of public title | Examples of what it does in public (ideas) |
|---|---|
| **Respectable** (a good keeper, a fair boss) | Merchants give her better prices; the chapel greets her; the guild takes her seriously |
| **Fighter** (brawl wins) | Troublemakers think twice before starting something; soldiers and orcs respect her |
| **Lewd** (earned in the taproom or upstairs) | Guests call her by it and reach sooner; Lust guests seek her out; the chapel turns its back; the Warm House wants her |
| **Famous** (fame and fans) | Strangers recognise her in the street; Zane wants to film her; nobles come to look |

**For the comedy:** a hero, a guest or a goblin merchant greets her loudly by her worst title in the middle of the square.

**To decide when titles are built out:** which titles are public, and what each one does. Each one needs its effect written in `titles.ts`.

## 13. Daily life

- **Seasons:** snow closes the high pass half the year. Winter thins out the farmers and brings fugitives and stranded travellers (STORY.md already uses this).
- **Festivals:**
  - **Bram's Fair** (late summer): dice, contests, drinking all day in the square. The busiest night of the year.
  - **The Field Blessing** (spring): the Mother's old planting rite at the edges of the fields. For couples, and an excuse.
  - **Veyra's Night** (midwinter, the longest night): the Warm House opens its doors to everyone.
  - **Oath Day** (autumn): the Stone-Hearted's day. Vows are renewed at the chapel, and tolls paid at the keep.
