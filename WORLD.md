## 1. The one-line world

**An adult comedy** (section 7d) **in a medieval fantasy world of many kingdoms and peoples (humans, elves, dwarves, orcs, beastfolk, goblins) that has modern things.** Castles, barons, knights in armour, adventurers' guilds, gods and monsters, the way an isekai world works. But people carry **phones**, wear **modern clothes**, take photos and send messages. A knight in plate checks his phone at the bar, next to an orc mercenary and a goblin selling phone charms. A farmer's daughter in a crop top and jeans sells turnips under the keep's walls.

The game never explains it as strange. To the people of Tarven it's just how the world is.

**The feel:** the modern things sit *on top* of the medieval world. They don't replace it. The keep is still stone, the road is still mud, the knight still fights with a sword.

## 3. How the modern things work

The isekai answer: **everything modern runs on mana-stones**. Small glowing crystals power phones, lights and cold boxes. They're mined and charged in the lowlands and sold up the pass.

The mana-stones turn the modern things into story:
- **The Baron owns the relay stone on the pass.** Every phone message in Tarven goes through his keep, and Pell reads them. So people who have secrets **write them on paper**. That's why Wren kept a paper ledger and wrote letters, and why the red book is a book. "Never text what you can whisper" is a Tarven saying.
- **Mana-stones cost money.** Farmers have cheap, cracked phones. Nobles have fine ones. A dead phone is a poor man's problem.
- **The high pass has bad signal.** Up on the quarry road and past the gate, phones go quiet. Fugitives and smugglers like it there.
- **Monsters and old ruins mess with mana.** In the ruins on the north road, phones die. Adventurers still carry swords, because a sword never runs out of charge.

**Guns:** none, or rare and useless (mana-stones can't power them, and powder fails in the mountain cold). Knights and swords stay the real power.

## 7. The kingdoms and the map

The world has **many kingdoms and many peoples**: humans, elves, dwarves, orcs, beastfolk and goblins. **All of them are people.** They think, trade, love, lie and drink. Goblins and orcs aren't monsters to be cleared out of a cave; they're neighbours, rivals and customers. (The real monsters are in section 12.)

| Kingdom *(working names)* | People | What it's like | To Tarven |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aldmere** (the Crown) | Mostly humans, every people as a minority | The big human kingdom. Barons hold its regions; the Crown is far away and slow | Tarven's own kingdom. Hesk and Varn are its barons |
| **Sylvaran** | Elves | Old forest courts, long lives, long memories. They look down on short-lived peoples, and secretly love their fashions | Elf nobles and envoys travel the pass; elf mages and archers join adventuring parties |
| **The Deephold** | Dwarves | Mountain cities under the range Tarven sits on. Miners, smiths, and the biggest mana-stone mines | The quarry is half dwarf-worked. Dwarf traders haul stones and steel down the pass |
| **The Khanate of Grask** | Orcs | Horse clans of the steppe past the east. Proud, blunt, loud. They sell their swords to whoever pays | Orc mercenaries fight on **both** sides of the war in the east, and drink in Tarven on the way |
| **The Wildmarches** | Beastfolk (wolf, cat, fox, bear, rabbit and other kin) | Free tribes and river towns in the forests south of the lowlands. Every kin its own customs | Beastfolk hunters, adventurers and runaways; some work Silas's routes |
| **The Cog Towns** | Goblins | Clever, quick, crowded trading cities on the lowland rivers. **They build the modern things**: goblin workshops turn dwarven mana-stones into phones, lamps and speakers | Goblin merchants sell phones and stones in the square; goblin smugglers out-haggle Silas |
| **The east** | An empire of humans *(not named yet)* | The enemy in the long war Father went into and Ash deserted from | Soldiers going east, deserters coming back |

**Tarven on the map:** the pass is the one road over the mountains between the lowlands of Aldmere and the war in the east, with the Deephold under the peaks beside it. That's why every people passes through the tavern.

| Place near Tarven | What it is | Who holds it |
|---|---|---|
| **The lowlands** | Farms, river towns, goblin workshops and markets | Baroness Isolde Varn |
| **The pass** | Toll gates, snow half the year, the relay stone on the keep | Baron Hesk |
| **Tarven** | The market town (`TOWN_DAY.md`) | The Baron, through Pell and the garrison |
| **The Deephold gate** | A dwarven door in the mountain, up the quarry road | The Deephold |
| **The north road** | Ruins older than any kingdom, monsters and treasure. Phones die there | Nobody: adventurers and bandits |

## 7b. The peoples

**Every one of them is an adult when they appear in the game (18+), and looks it.** That matters most for the short peoples. Goblins and dwarves are short, but they're built as grown-ups: adult faces, adult proportions, stubble or lines, broad or wiry builds. They're never childlike in body, face or writing (GAME_CONCEPT.md §7.1).

| People | Look | Character | In the tavern |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Humans** | As now | Every kind | Most guests in Chapter 1 |
| **Elves** | Tall and slim, long pointed ears | Graceful, proud, patient; live for centuries | Big tippers who sneer at the ale; nobles, mages, archers |
| **Dwarves** | Short and very broad, big beards (men and many women), strong hands | Stubborn, loyal, love a drinking contest | Drink the most, brawl the hardest, pay in stones and silver |
| **Orcs** | Big and heavy, green to grey skin, tusks | Blunt, honest, loud, openly lustful, a warrior's pride | Soldiers and mercenaries; never refuse a fight or a dare |
| **Beastfolk** | Human bodies with their kin's ears and tail (cat, wolf, fox, bear, rabbit), some with more fur | Each kin differs: wolves loyal, cats proud, foxes sly, rabbits restless | Hunters, adventurers, runaways; a heat season raises their Lust (a mood, not a reason to drop consent) |
| **Goblins** | Short, wiry, green, big ears, sharp grins | Clever, quick-talking, shameless hagglers, mad for gadgets | Merchants and smugglers; always selling something; pay in gadgets |

**What each people thinks of sex** (for the writing):
- **Elves:** slow and elaborate. They court for a season, so a quick tumble in a tavern is a scandal to them (and a thrill).
- **Dwarves:** private behind closed doors, boasting in the bar afterwards.
- **Orcs:** open and direct. They ask straight out, take a no straight, and don't understand shame.
- **Beastfolk:** depends on the kin. Heat seasons are spoken of openly.
- **Goblins:** everything is a deal, including that, and they'll haggle over it.
- **Humans:** everything in between, and the Church of Veyra knows it.

**For the game:**
- **Variety rule:** the peoples are the best answer to "never one body recoloured". A party of four can be a human, an elf, an orc and a goblin.
- **Factions keep their jobs, the peoples mix:** an orc soldier in the garrison, a goblin smuggler in Silas's crew, an elf noble, a dwarf adventurer, a fox beastfolk fugitive.
- **Art, cheapest first:**
  1. Elves: ears on the existing heads.
  2. Beastfolk: ears and a tail, like the bunny set's.
  3. Orcs: skin tones, tusks, the heavy body sliders.
  4. Dwarves: the existing bodies short and broad, plus beards.
  5. Goblins: a new small adult body, the biggest job.
- **Prejudice is a story tool:** some guests sneer at orcs or goblins. How she treats them moves those peoples' fans, and Respect with the bigots.

More kingdoms, more heroes, and more trouble. The gods have a sense of humour, so the Gift rarely goes to the person anyone would have picked.

**Every hero is an adult (18+).**

| Hero *(working names)* | Kingdom | Gift | The joke |

Meant to win the war in the east; has been "preparing" in her taproom for three months on the Crown's tab, which the Crown never pays. The King had his own reasons for wanting a hero with stamina (section 8) |

| **Yumi** | The Khanate of Grask (orcs) | *Command Voice* | The smallest, shyest, most polite orc in the clans. They take every quiet "um, please stop" as sacred law |
| **Mother Reyes** | The Wildmarches (beastfolk) | *Heal Anything* | A cat-kin healer who treats every patient, king or beggar, like a sick goat. Nobody dares argue |

**In the game:**
- **Guests:** heroes come through the tavern as named regulars, each with their own lines, wants and trouble. They're perfect for the variety rule.
- **Their Gifts make comedy scenes:** Kevin's stamina at the after-hours games, Zane filming her strip dare (*Go Viral*: huge Fame, a huge Respect hit), Yumi accidentally ordering a whole orc warband to behave.
- **Rival hero drama:** heroes from rival kingdoms meet in her taproom, and it ends in a brawl, a drinking contest or a bet.
- **Romance and adult routes:** every hero can have one (all adults), under the usual consent rule.
- **Story hooks:** the Crown wants Kevin to finally go east; Old Hollis could audit the Baron's books (another road to the red book); the Hero of the East is coming over the pass.

## 7d. Tone: an adult comedy

**Last Call at Tarven is a comedy.** Crude, absurd and messed up, and sexual. The world takes ridiculous things completely seriously: a church of sex, a goblin selling phones to a knight, a kingdom's Hero living on the Crown's tab in a bar.

- **The jokes come out of the systems:** a title the whole town calls her (section 9), a photo that goes viral, a drunk Hero's Gift backfiring, the Baron's men reading everyone's texts.
- **Everyone is a little ridiculous**, the villains too. Pell and the Baron are scheming, greedy and petty, and funny with it.
- **A few sincere beats stay:** a romance that lands, Wren's letters, Father in the east. Comedy works better with a little heart.
- **The house rules don't change:** every character is an adult, and the consent switch (`GAME_PATH.MD`) works the same in a joke as anywhere else.

**King Edmund the Generous** *(working name, about 52)* has a cuckold fetish. Nothing makes him happier than his wife in another man's bed, and he runs the kingdom the same way: generous, hands-off, and secretly delighted when someone else does the work. He picked Kevin as Hero mostly for the *Infinite Stamina*, with the Queen in mind. Kevin still hasn't worked that out.

**Queen Seraphine** *(working name, about 41)* is sexually frustrated and a masochist. The King only ever wants to watch, and the whole court treats her like porcelain. What she wants is to be scolded, pinned, spanked and put in her place, and nobody at court dares. So she sneaks out in disguise to places where people don't know who she is.

**How they come into the game (comedy, all consensual, all adults):**

- **What the heroine can do:** help the King's wish, become the Queen's favourite person to be humiliated by, sell the secret, or all three. A royal favour is worth more than the Baron's whole keep, which is a funny new way to win the Act 3 plot.
- **Gossip:** the town feed is full of "a lady who looks EXACTLY like the Queen" sightings. Nobody believes it.

## 9. Titles in public (no status plates)

**There are no status plates**: nobody can see anyone's stats or skills. Her stats, skills and passives are hers alone, in her journal.

**Titles are different:** a title (`titles.ts`, Karryn's Prison style) is **what people start calling her**, so some titles spread out into the town. They travel by gossip and the town feed, the way any reputation does.

- **Private titles** only work for her, as now (the bonus and the effect in `titles.ts`).
- **Public titles** also change how the world treats her while she wears one. Guests use the title to her face, strangers in the street know it, and some places open or close.

| Kind of public title | Examples of what it does in public (ideas) |
|---|---|
| **Respectable** (a good keeper, a fair boss) | Merchants give her better prices; the chapel greets her; the guild takes her seriously |
| **Fighter** (brawl wins) | Troublemakers think twice before starting something; soldiers and orcs respect her |
| **Lewd** (earned in the taproom or upstairs) | Guests call her by it and reach sooner; Lust guests seek her out; the chapel turns its back; the Warm House wants her |
| **Famous** (fame and fans) | Strangers recognise her in the street; Zane wants to film her; nobles come to look |

**For the comedy:** a hero, a guest or a goblin merchant greets her loudly by her worst title in the middle of the square.

**To decide when titles are built out:** which titles are public, and what each one does. Each one needs its effect written in `titles.ts`.

## 11. Magic

- **Mana is in everything**, but most people can't cast. They use **mana-stones**: phones, lamps, cold boxes.
- **Casters** are rare and trained: guild mages, chapel healers, a few hedge witches. The mage and cleric guest outfits are already in the game.
- **No magic replaces the sword.** Spells are slow, cost a lot and fail near the ruins. Knights and fighters still win most fights. Brawls in the tavern are fists, not fireballs.
- **Everyday adult magic, kept small:**
  - mana-stone toys (the Church of Veyra's)
  - a charm against pregnancy, sold at every chapel and at the Warm House, so nobody has to think about it
  - love philtres sold under the counter, and the trouble they cause

## 13. Daily life

- **Seasons:** snow closes the high pass half the year. Winter thins out the farmers and brings fugitives and stranded travellers (STORY.md already uses this).
- **Festivals:**
  - **Bram's Fair** (late summer): dice, contests, drinking all day in the square. The busiest night of the year.
  - **The Field Blessing** (spring): the Mother's old planting rite at the edges of the fields. For couples, and an excuse.
  - **Veyra's Night** (midwinter, the longest night): the Warm House opens its doors to everyone.
  - **Oath Day** (autumn): the Stone-Hearted's day. Vows are renewed at the chapel, and tolls paid at the keep.
- **The town feed** *(working name: the Board)*: everyone's posts, photos and rumours in one place, all through the Baron's relay. It's where gossip lives by day.
- **Fashion by class:**
  - farmers mix old smocks with cheap modern clothes
  - soldiers wear armour on duty and hoodies off it
  - smugglers dress dark and modern
  - nobles wear tailored modern coats in their old house colours
  - Veyra's devotees wear red
- **Music:** a bard with a lute, and a mana-stone speaker playing lowland songs, often in the same tavern.
